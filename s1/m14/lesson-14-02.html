★ Supine Stem plus -ūrus
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Yesterday you learned what the FAP is. Today you learn to build one.
The rule is short: take the supine stem (the same stem you already use for the PPP) and add -ūrus / -ūra / -ūrum. Because that ending follows the ordinary 2nd declension adjective pattern, the FAP is far simpler to decline than the PAP — no 3rd-declension irregularity, just regular -us / -a / -um agreement.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to find a verb’s supine stem and add -ūrus to build the FAP.
2
I will be able to decline the FAP as a regular 2nd declension adjective in all genders and cases.
3
I will be able to recognize the key irregular FAPs, especially futūrus (from sum).

📚 PART 2 · FIND THE STEM, ADD THE ENDING 14 min

The supine stem is the starting point

The Future Active Participle is formed from the supine stem (also called the perfect passive participial stem, or PPP stem) plus the ending -ūrus / -ūra / -ūrum. Find the PPP, drop its -us to get the stem, then add -ūrus. Here it is across all four conjugations, plus the two irregulars worth memorizing:

Conj.VerbPPPSupine stemFAP (m. / f. / n.)Meaning
1stamō, amāreamātusamāt-amātūrus / amātūra / amātūrumabout to love
2ndmoneō, monēremonitusmonit-monitūrus / monitūra / monitūrumabout to warn
3rdmittō, mitteremissusmiss-missūrus / missūra / missūrumabout to send
4thaudiō, audīreaudītusaudīt-audītūrus / audītūra / audītūrumabout to hear
Irreg.sum, esse(no PPP)fut- (special)futūrus / futūra / futūrumabout to be
Irreg.eō, īre(itum)it-itūrus / itūra / itūrumabout to go
💡 ONE FAMOUS IRREGULAR

The verb sum has no PPP, but it does have a FAP: futūrus — “about to be.” You already know its descendant: the English word future is literally “the thing about to be.”

📌 PART 3 · DECLINING THE FAP 7 min

It agrees like bonus, -a, -um

Unlike the PAP (3rd declension, stem in -nt-), the FAP follows the familiar 2nd declension adjective pattern. It agrees with the noun it modifies in gender, number, and case. Apply standard endings after the -ūr- base:

CaseMasc.Fem.Neut.
Nom. sg.casūruscasūracasūrum
Gen. sg.casūrīcasūraecasūrī
Dat. sg.casūrōcasūraecasūrō
Acc. sg.casūrumcasūramcasūrum

The only real work is finding the supine stem in the first place. Once you have -ūr-, every ending is one you already know.
