📚 PART 2 · THE SIX TENSES 15 min

All six, using amō, amāre

The table below summarizes all six active indicative tenses using amō, amāre (1st conjugation) as the model. The 1st person singular form is given for reference; what matters is recognizing the tense from its stem or ending pattern anywhere in a passage.

Tense1sg formKey markerMeaning
PresentamōPresent stem + personal endings (-ō, -s, -t…)I love / I am loving / I do love
Imperfectamābam-bā- between stem and endingI was loving / I used to love
Futureamābō-b- + future vowel (-ō/-is/-it… or -am/-ēs/-et…)I will love
PerfectamāvīPerfect stem + -ī, -istī, -it, -imus, -istis, -ēruntI loved / I have loved
PluperfectamāveramPerfect stem + -eram, -erās, -erat…I had loved
Future perf.amāverōPerfect stem + -erō, -eris, -erit…I will have loved
💡 TWO SYSTEMS, ONE VERB

Notice the split. The top three tenses (present, imperfect, future) build on the present stem amā-. The bottom three (perfect, pluperfect, future perfect) build on the perfect stem amāv-. Spot which stem a verb is using and you've already narrowed it to three tenses.

Out loud: the form amābāmus shows the marker -bā-. What tense and person is it, and how would you translate it? Say your answer, then reveal.

Imperfect, 1st person plural: “we were loving” (or “we used to love”). The -bā- is the imperfect marker, and -mus is the “we” ending. The imperfect always describes ongoing or repeated past action.
