📚 PART 2 · WHO WAS OVID? 14 min

The poet behind the whole year

Publius Ovidius Naso — Ovid — lived from 43 BCE to 17 or 18 CE. He wrote under the emperor Augustus, in the same generation as Virgil and Horace, during the golden age of Latin poetry. Where Virgil wrote the solemn national epic of Rome, Ovid was wittier, faster, and more playful. His masterpiece is the Metamorphōsēs — a single long poem in fifteen books that gathers together dozens of Greek and Roman myths, all linked by one idea: transformation. People become trees, stars, stones, animals, rivers, and flowers.

This poem is the spine of both semesters of Latin 2. Nearly every story you read this year comes from it. Knowing what Ovid was doing — retelling the world's myths as an unbroken chain of changes — makes every passage more legible before you translate a single word.

📜 THE TITLE ITSELF

The Greek word metamorphōsis is built from meta- ("change") + morphē ("form, shape"). It literally means "a change of shape." English borrows it directly: a caterpillar's metamorphosis into a butterfly is exactly Ovid's subject — one form becoming another. The Latin word for that shape, forma, is one of this module's five vocabulary words.

💡 WHY START WITH A POET?

In Latin 1 you read short made-up sentences. In Latin 2 you read adapted real literature. Grammar stops being the point and becomes the tool. Ovid is the author who makes that shift worth it — his stories are strange, vivid, and unforgettable.
