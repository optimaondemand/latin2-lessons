★ The Passive Voice, Gathered in One Place
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
You met the passive tense by tense across the semester. Today they line up together — all six, in one view.
The passive voice was the spine of Semester 1, and it is the largest single block on the final. Today's review sorts it into its two natural halves — the present system (present, imperfect, future), built on personal endings, and the perfect system (perfect, pluperfect, future perfect), built from the fourth principal part plus a form of esse. Then we settle the “by” problem: agent vs. means. No new grammar — just everything you already know, put in order.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to recognize and form the passive in all six tenses of the present and perfect systems.
2
I will be able to translate each passive tense with the right English (is/was being/will be/has been/had been/will have been).
3
I will be able to tell personal agent (ā/ab + ablative) apart from means (plain ablative).
⏰ PART 1 · RECALL 4 min

Active vs. passive, in one line

The voice tells you whether the subject does the action or receives it. Same event, two viewpoints:

Deā nympham mūtat.

The goddess changes the nymph. — active: the subject dea does the changing.

Nympha ā deā mūtātur.

The nymph is changed by the goddess. — passive: the subject nympha receives the action.

📚 PART 2 · THE TWO HALVES OF THE PASSIVE 14 min

Present system: personal endings

The present, imperfect, and future passive are built by swapping the active personal endings for the passive personal endings -r, -ris, -tur, -mur, -minī, -ntur. Here is amāre across the three present-system tenses (3rd sg. shown for each):

TenseForm (3rd sg.)English
Present passiveamāturhe/she/it is loved
Imperfect passiveamābāturhe/she/it was being loved
Future passiveamābiturhe/she/it will be loved

Here is the full present passive of amāre so you can see all six endings at work:

SingularPlural
1stamor — I am lovedamāmur — we are loved
2ndamāris — you are lovedamāminī — you all are loved
3rdamātur — he/she/it is lovedamantur — they are loved

Perfect system: PPP + esse

The perfect, pluperfect, and future perfect passive are built two words at a time: the fourth principal part (the perfect passive participle, e.g. amātus, -a, -um) plus a form of esse. The participle carries the meaning; the esse form sets the tense.

TenseForm (masc. sg.)English
Perfect passiveamātus esthe has been / was loved
Pluperfect passiveamātus erathe had been loved
Future perfect passiveamātus erithe will have been loved
💡 THE ONE THING STUDENTS MISS

In the perfect system the participle is an adjective — it must agree with the subject in gender, number, and case. Nympha mūtāta est (she was changed) but Nymphae mūtātae sunt (they, feminine, were changed). Watch that ending on the participle.

📌 PART 3 · THE “BY” PROBLEM 7 min

Agent vs. means

A passive verb often names who or what did the action. Latin marks the two differently:

Personal agent — a person

ā/ab + ablative
Nympha ā deā mūtātur.
The nymph is changed by the goddess.

Means / instrument — a thing

plain ablative (no preposition)
Puer sagittā vulnerātur.
The boy is wounded by an arrow.

Quick test: could it act on its own will? A person can (ā/ab). A tool cannot (plain ablative). Both translate as “by” in English — Latin keeps them apart.

One more, out loud: how do you build any perfect-system passive, and what does the esse form decide? Say your rule, then reveal.

Take the fourth principal part (the perfect passive participle, agreeing with the subject) and add a form of esse. The esse form sets the tense: present est → perfect, imperfect erat → pluperfect, future erit → future perfect. So mūtāta est / erat / erit = she was / had been / will have been changed.
