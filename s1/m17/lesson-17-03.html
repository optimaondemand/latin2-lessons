📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
A participle is a verb wearing an adjective's clothes — and this semester you met three of them.
A participle lets you pack a whole idea — “the man who had been warned,” “the girl fleeing,” “about to speak” — into a single adjective-like word. The final tests all three: the PPP (perfect passive participle), the PAP (present active participle), and the FAP (future active participle). Today they sit side by side so you can tell them apart by voice (active or passive) and time (before, same, or after the main verb). No new grammar — one clean chart and plenty of practice.

📚 PART 2 · THE ONE CHART 16 min

All three, side by side

Each participle differs on two axes — voice and time — and each has a sign you can spot on sight. Model verb: mūtāre (to change).

ParticipleSignVoiceTimeTranslation
PPP — perfect passive-tus/-sus (4th p.p.)passivebeforehaving been changed / changed
PAP — present active-ns, -ntisactivesamechanging / while changing
FAP — future active-ūrusactiveafterabout to change / going to change

And the actual forms for mūtāre:

ParticipleFormed fromForm
PPP4th principal partmūtātus, -a, -um
PAPpresent stem + -nsmūtāns, mūtāntis
FAPPPP stem + -ūrusmūtātūrus, -a, -um
💡 THE QUICK TEST

See -ns? Present active (-ing). See -ūrus? Future active (about to). Everything else that looks like the 4th principal part is the PPP — passive, and already done (having been ___). Two signs to memorize; the third is what's left.

📌 PART 3 · FOLDED INTO A SENTENCE 6 min

Reading a participial phrase

A participle carries its own little clause. Find the noun it agrees with, then unfold it into English — often with “who,” “while,” or “after.”

Nympha, ā deā mūtāta, arborem fīt.

The nymph, having been changed by the goddess, becomes a tree. — mūtāta = PPP (passive, before), agreeing with nympha.

Apollō nympham fugiēntem videt.

Apollo sees the fleeing nymph. — fugiēntem = PAP (active, same time), agreeing with nympham.

Nympha, arborem futūra, currit.

The nymph, about to become a tree, runs. — futūra = FAP (active, after), agreeing with nympha.
