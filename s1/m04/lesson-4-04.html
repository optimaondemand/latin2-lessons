⏰ PART 1 · THE PASSAGE 18 min

Narcissus et Aqua

Read the passage aloud, one line at a time. For each line, find the verb first, decide whether it is active or passive, then look for an agent (ā/ab + ablative) or a means (plain ablative). Use the glossary below as you go.

AFTER OVID, METAMORPHŌSĒS III · ADAPTED FOR LATIN 2
1In valle umbrōsā fōns aquae limpidae erat. Ad hunc locum Narcissus, ab omnibus nymphis amātus sed ā nūllā acceptus, vēnit. 2In aquā imāgō pulcherrima vidēbātur. Imāgō ā Narcissō spectābātur et mīrābātur — sed cuius erat haec imāgō? 3Corpus suum ab ipsō Narcissō nōn agnōscēbātur. Flammā novā ūrēbātur: nōn ā deā, nōn ab homine — sed ā sē ipsō. 4Quotiēns aqua tangēbātur, imāgō fugēbat. Narcissus misellus repellēbātur semper ab eā quam amābat.
📚 GLOSSARY

valle umbrōsā — in a shadowy valley · limpidae — clear (gen. sg.) · nūllā acceptus — accepted by none · pulcherrima — most beautiful · cuius — whose? (interrogative gen.) · agnōscō, -ere — to recognize · ā sē ipsō — by himself (reflexive agent) · quotiēns — whenever · tangēbātur — was touched · misellus — poor, wretched (diminutive of miser) · eā quam amābat — her whom he loved

📚 PART 2 · THE WORD BEHIND THE STORY 8 min
🏛️ FROM MYTH TO VOCABULARY · NARCISSISM

The word narcissism comes directly from this story. In the 20th century, psychologists borrowed Narcissus's name to describe excessive self-absorption — a person so fixed on his own image that he cannot truly love anyone else. Ovid gave us the myth; the myth gave us the vocabulary.

This is not the only time an Ovidian character became a permanent word in modern languages. Echo, narcissism, hermaphrodite, mentor, and dozens more all trace back to the Metamorphōsēs. When you read Ovid, you are reading the source of words you already use.

In your own words: translate line 4 — Narcissus misellus repellēbātur semper ab eā quam amābat. Work it out, then reveal one correct version.

“Poor Narcissus was always being driven back by the one (her) whom he loved.” — repellēbātur is imperfect passive (“was being driven back”); ab eā is the agent (“by her”), and quam amābat is the relative clause “whom he loved” — the reflection he could never hold.
