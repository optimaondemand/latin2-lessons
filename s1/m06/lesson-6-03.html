📚 PART 1 · EIGHT NEW WORDS 10 min

Mōdulus VI vocabulary

All eight words appear in this module’s Latin passage (tomorrow). Study them now. The Vocabulary Check will test six of these plus four drawn from the cumulative M01–M05 bank.

LatinPart of speechMeaning
certāmen, certāminisn.contest, competition
ōlimadv.once, formerly, at some time

arānea, -aef.spider, spider’s web
vincō, vincere, vīcīverbto conquer, win, defeat

📜 NOTES ON THESE WORDS

Certāmen is a 3rd-declension neuter noun; its nominative and accusative singular are identical. Īra appears in the passage as the cause of Minerva’s action — the English word irate is its descendant. Watch the two verbs with irregular perfect stems: vincō → vīc- (so “had conquered” = vīcerat) and cadō → cecid- (so “had fallen” = ceciderat). And arānea gives us the whole zoological order Araneae — the spiders.

📌 PART 2 · THE PLUPERFECT AT WORK 10 min

The tense of backstory

Use the pluperfect when one past event had already finished before another past event began. In Arachnē’s story, the contest’s outcome matters because of what had already happened before Minerva’s anger struck.

Antequam Minerva vēnit, Arachnē tēlam texuerat.

Before Minerva came, Arachnē had woven the web. — the weaving was already complete when Minerva arrived.

Minerva Arachnēn saepe monuerat.

Minerva had often warned Arachnē. — the warnings came before the contest.

Virgō deae opus superāverat.

The maiden had surpassed the goddess’s work. — already true before Minerva reacted.

💡 STORY CONNECTION

Before the contest began, Minerva had warned Arachnē (monuerat). By the time of the transformation, Arachnē had already woven a perfect tapestry (texuerat). The pluperfect tells you what happened earlier in the sequence — the grammatical way of saying “this was already done, and done before everything else unfolded.”

✅ PART 3 · PLUPERFECT, OR NOT? 15 min

Tell the tenses apart

The pluperfect active has a signature: perfect stem + -eram/-erās/-erat…. Watch out for look-alikes — the imperfect passive -bātur, the present passive -tur, and the plain perfect. Five reps; wrong answers give a hint, and Reset lets you try again.

✅ TRY IT · identify the form
Arachnē tēlam texuerat. The highlighted verb is…
✅ TRY IT · not pluperfect
Minerva ā Iove laudātur. This form is…
✅ TRY IT · identify the form
Dea multās nymphās monuerat. This form is…
✅ TRY IT · the tricky one
Arachnē in arāneam mūtābātur. This form is…
✅ TRY IT · build from the irregular stem
Vincō has perfect stem vīc-. Which is “she had conquered”?

In your notes, contrast mūtāverat and mūtābātur: which is pluperfect active, and what tells you? Say your reasoning, then reveal.

Mūtāverat is pluperfect active (“had changed”): perfect stem mūtāv- + erat. Mūtābātur is imperfect passive (“was being changed”): present stem mūtā- + -bātur. The clue is the middle: -v- plus -era- signals the pluperfect; -bā- plus -tur signals the imperfect passive.
