📚 PART 1 · THE MASTER TABLE 10 min

All six passive tenses of amāre

Study this carefully — it is your reference for every Latin text from here forward. Notice the split: the first three build with passive endings; the last three build with PPP + a form of esse.

Tense3rd sg. m. formPatternMeaning

🏆 MILESTONE

This module marks the end of the passive indicative system. You now have all six tenses of the passive — exactly mirroring the six active tenses from Latin 1. From Module 11 onward, Latin 2 introduces the participial system (verbal adjectives): the PPP you already know, the Present Active Participle (M11), and the Future Active Participle (M14). The passive voice is the foundation on which they all rest.

📌 PART 2 · WHERE THE FUTURE PERFECT LIVES 6 min

Postquam and cum + future clauses

When a future event must be completed before another future event happens, Latin uses the future perfect in the first clause. English is looser — it often uses the simple future or present for both — but Latin is precise. Watch for this structure in tomorrow's passage.

Postquam Mīdās lavātus erit, maledictum solūtum erit.

After Midas will have been washed, the curse will have been lifted. — Both events are future, but the washing must complete first, so the first clause is future perfect.

Eight new words

These eight words appear in tomorrow's passage and recur in later modules. Study them now.

💡 NOTES

Paenitēt is impersonal — no personal subject. The idiom mē paenitet (literally “it repents me”) = “I am sorry / I repent.” Aureus is a 2-1-2 adjective like bonus. Aetās is 3rd declension; genitive aetātis.
