⏰ PART 1 · READ THE PASSAGE 14 min

Mīdās Lavātur

AFTER OVID, METAMORPHŌSĒS XI — ADAPTED FOR LATIN 2
1Mīdās rēx, quī omnia in aurum mūtāverat, nunc flēbat: cibus aureus erat, aqua aurea erat, fīlia aurea erat.
2Mīdās miserābilis ad Bacchum supplicāvit et ōrāvit: “Deus magne, mē paenitet dōnī tuī! Hoc maledictum ā mē petītum est — sed nunc perīre volō.”
3Bacchus deus, ā rēge ōrātus, miserātus est: “Mīdā, ad fluvium Pactōlum vādē et in aquā lavāre!”
4Mīdās statim ad fluvium cucurrit. Postquam in aquā lavātus erit, maledictum solūtum erit.
5Mīdās in fluvium ingressus est et corpus suum lāvit. Aurum ā corpore in harēnam fluviī flūxit.
6Ubi Mīdās ē fluviō exiit, harēna aurea in rīpā relicta erat — maledictum solūtum erat, rēx līberātus erat.
7Harēna aurea in fluviō Pactōlō per multās aetātēs vīsa est: aurum ex illō diē ibi inventum est.
8Mīdās, quī ōlim aureum dōnum optāverat, nunc dīvitiās spernēbat; paupertās, nōn aurum, ab eō optāta erit.
Glossary:  miserābilis — wretched, pitiable (nom. sg.) · dōnī tuī — of your gift (gen. sg. after paenitet) · perīre — to perish, die (inf. of pereō) · miserātus est — took pity (deponent perfect, miseror) · vādē — go! (imperative of vādō) · lavāre — wash yourself! (pres. passive imperative, reflexive) · ingressus est — entered (deponent perfect, ingredior) · rīpā — on the bank (abl. of rīpa) · spernēbat — was scorning (impf. of spernō) · paupertās — poverty (nom. sg.)
🏛️ THE HISTORICAL PACTOLUS

The river Pactolus in Lydia (modern Turkey) genuinely carried gold-bearing deposits in antiquity. The wealth of King Croesus — the proverbially rich Lydian king — was thought to derive from it. Ovid's myth offers a poetic explanation: the river runs with gold dust because Midas washed his curse away in its waters. It is a characteristic move of the Metamorphōsēs — the myth does not just entertain, it explains the world as Greeks and Romans observed it.

💡 PART 2 · PRIUS TEMPTĀ · TRY BEFORE YOU LOOK 10 min

Translate each sentence and identify the underlined passive form — including its tense. The third introduces the future perfect passive. Try each before you reveal.

1. Mīdās ā Bacchō iuvābitur.
2. Aurum in fluviō relictum erat.
3. Postquam Mīdās lāverit, maledictum solūtum erit.

1. iuvābitur — “Midas will be helped by Bacchus.” Future passive, 3rd sg (iuvā- + bi + tur).

2. relictum erat — “Gold had been left in the river.” Pluperfect passive, 3rd sg n. — PPP relictum (agrees with aurum) + erat (impf. of esse).

3. solūtum erit — “After Midas has washed, the curse will have been lifted.” Future perfect passive, 3rd sg n. — PPP solūtum (agrees with maledictum) + erit (fut. of esse). This is the new tense.
