📚 PART 2 · THE BIG IDEA 12 min

One pattern, six tenses

The Latin passive splits into two halves. The first three tenses — present, imperfect, future — are built with passive personal endings right on the verb (amātur, amābātur, amābitur). The last three — perfect, pluperfect, and this week's future perfect — are built a different way: a Perfect Passive Participle (PPP) plus a form of esse.

Tenses 1–3 — endings

amātur — he is loved
amābātur — he was being loved
amābitur — he will be loved

Built with passive endings on the verb stem.

Tenses 4–6 — PPP + esse

amātus est — he was loved
amātus erat — he had been loved
amātus erit — he will have been loved

Built with a participle plus a form of esse.

The whole trick of the second group is this: the PPP stays the same; you change the form of esse to change the tense. Perfect uses est / sunt (present of esse); pluperfect uses erat / erant (imperfect of esse). This week's future perfect uses the future of esse.

💡 PRIUS CŌGITĀ · THINK BEFORE YOU START

The future tense of esse is erō, eris, erit, erimus, eritis, erunt. If perfect is PPP + est, and pluperfect is PPP + erat, then future perfect will be PPP + erit. You can almost predict tomorrow's whole lesson from that one line.
