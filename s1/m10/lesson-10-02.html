★ The Sixth Tense — Amātus Erō
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Yesterday you predicted it. Today you build it: PPP + erit and the whole future perfect passive falls into place.
The future perfect passive follows the same building pattern as the perfect and pluperfect: a Perfect Passive Participle plus a form of esse. The only change is which form of esse you attach — this time the future of esse: erō, eris, erit, erimus, eritis, erunt. Learn the paradigm and two rules today, and you will have every tense of the Latin passive indicative at your command.

📚 PART 2 · THE PARADIGM 14 min

Future perfect passive of amāre

Take the PPP amātus and attach the future tense of esse. That is the entire future perfect passive. The participle changes ending to agree with its subject (here, -us in the singular, -ī in the plural, for a masculine subject).

PersonFuture perfect passive (m.)Meaning
1st sgamātus erōI will have been loved
2nd sgamātus erisyou will have been loved
3rd sgamātus erithe/she/it will have been loved
1st plamātī erimuswe will have been loved
2nd plamātī eritisyou (pl.) will have been loved
3rd plamātī eruntthey will have been loved
💡 NOTHING NEW TO MEMORIZE

You already know the PPP (Module 08) and the future of esse (Latin 1). The future perfect passive is simply those two familiar pieces joined together — no new endings at all.

📌 PART 3 · THE TWO RULES 8 min

How the form behaves

RULE 1 · THE PPP AGREES WITH THE SUBJECT

The PPP still agrees with the subject in gender, number, and case — exactly as in the perfect and pluperfect passives. What changes is only the form of esse attached to it.

Via mūnīta erit.

The road will have been built. — mūnīta (f. sg.) agrees with via.

Oppida capta erunt.

The towns will have been captured. — capta (n. pl.) agrees with oppida.

RULE 2 · THE FORM OF ESSE DETERMINES THE TENSE

The PPP stays constant; you change esse to change the tense. To read any PPP + esse form: identify the esse form first, establish the tense, then read the PPP for the passive meaning.

amātus est

he was loved — perfect passive (est = present of esse)

amātus erat

he had been loved — pluperfect passive (erat = imperfect of esse)

amātus erit

he will have been loved — future perfect passive (erit = future of esse)

portātus erit — “he will have been carried.” PPP portātus (m. sg.) + erit (future of esse). Change erit to erat and you would have the pluperfect; to est and you would have the perfect. The esse form is the whole difference.
