📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Yesterday you learned what the passive means. Today you learn how Latin spells it.
The present passive uses the same present stem you already know from Latin 1 — but it replaces the active personal endings with a new set of passive endings. Learn these six endings once and you can turn any 1st conjugation verb passive. This is the single most important pattern in the module.

💡 THE SIX PASSIVE ENDINGS

Learn these once: -or, -ris, -tur, -mur, -minī, -ntur. They replace the active endings in the present, imperfect, and future tenses, and they are the same for all conjugations — what changes across conjugations is only the stem vowel before the ending. This week, focus on the 1st conjugation amā- stem.

📌 PART 3 · THE ONE ODD FORM 6 min

Rule: amō → amor

The 1st person singular passive is formed by dropping the active -ō and adding -r. The result is amor.

amō → amor

I am loved — drop the -ō, add -r.

laudō → laudor

I am praised.

vulnerō → vulneror

I am wounded.

⚠️ A LOOK-ALIKE TO WATCH

The form amor is identical in spelling to the noun amor, amōris (love). Context always resolves the ambiguity. As a verb form in a paradigm or sentence, amor means “I am loved.”
