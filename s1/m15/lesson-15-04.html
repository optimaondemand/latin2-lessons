⏰ PART 1 · READ THE PASSAGE 16 min

Iō et Argus

Read each line aloud. Every bolded word is a participle — pause on each and name its type and the noun it modifies. Use the glossary below the passage.

After Ovid, Metamorphōsēs I — adapted for Latin 2
1Iuppiter, Iōnem vidēns, statim amāvit; nūbem obscūram circum eam posuit ut Iūnō nihil vidēret.
2Iūnō autem, nūbem suspicāta, ad terram dēscendit; Iuppiter, Iōnem mūtātūrus, eam in vaccam albam convertit.
3Iūnō vaccam pulchre mūtātam petīvit et Iovem ōrāvit ut sibi donum darētur; Iuppiter, nōn negātūrus, vaccam trādidit.
4Iūnō vaccam Iovī invīsam Argō custōdī trādidit; Argus centum oculōs habēbat et semper aliquī oculī vigilābant.
5Iō, ā custōde circumdata, in agrīs errābat; pater eius, Īnachus deus flūminis, eam nōn agnōscēbat.
6Iuppiter, Iōnis misertus, Mercurium mīsit; Mercurius, harundine canēns, ad Argum accessīt.
7Argus, fābulam audiēns, paulātim obdormiēbat; Mercurius fābulam longam nārrāvit donec omnēs centum oculī clausī erant.
8Mercurius, gladium strictūrus, Argum occīdit; Argus cecidit, et lūx ex centum oculīs exiit.
9Iūnō, Argō occīsō dolēns, oculōs eius in caudā pāvōnis posuit; pāvō ita plūmās magnificās habēbat.
10Iō autem, liberāta, in Aegyptum fūgit; ibi tandem in fōrmam hūmānam restitūta est et dea Aegyptī facta est.
Glossary:  vidēns — PAP of videō, seeing · nūbem obscūram — a dark cloud (acc. sg.) · nūbem suspicāta — PPP deponent, having suspected the cloud (from suspicor) · mūtātūrus — FAP nom. sg. m. of mūtō, about to change · pulchre mūtātam — PPP acc. sg. f. of mūtō, beautifully changed · nōn negātūrus — FAP nom. sg. m. of negō, not about to refuse · Iovī invīsam — PPP acc. sg. f. of invideō, hated by Jupiter · ā custōde circumdata — PPP nom. sg. f. of circumdō, surrounded by the guard · Īnachus — Inachus, river god, father of Io · agnōscēbat — was recognizing (impf. of agnōscō) · misertus — PPP deponent, having pitied (from misereor + gen.) · canēns — PAP nom. sg. of canō, playing, singing · audiēns — PAP nom. sg. of audiō, hearing · clausī — PPP nom. pl. m. of claudō, closed · gladium strictūrus — FAP nom. sg. m. of stringō, about to draw his sword · Argō occīsō — PPP abl. sg. m. of occīdō, Argus having been killed (ablative phrase) · caudā pāvōnis — on the tail of the peacock · liberāta — PPP nom. sg. f. of līberō, having been freed · restitūta est — perfect passive of restituō, was restored
📚 PART 2 · THE MYTH & ITS MEANING 8 min
🏛️ JUPITER, IO, JUNO, ARGUS, MERCURY

Jupiter falls for the nymph Io and hides the affair under a dark cloud. His wife Juno, suspicious of that too-convenient cloud, descends to earth — and Jupiter, caught, turns Io into a white heifer to conceal her. Juno, unfooled, asks for the “lovely” cow as a gift, and Jupiter, unwilling to give himself away by refusing, hands her over.

Juno sets the hundred-eyed giant Argus to guard Io; some of his eyes always stay awake. Jupiter sends Mercury, who lulls Argus with pipe-music and a long story until every eye closes — then kills him. Grieving, Juno sets Argus’s hundred eyes into the tail of her sacred bird, the peacock. Io, at last freed, flees to Egypt, is restored to human form, and becomes a goddess there.

📜 WHY THE PEACOCK HAS EYES

This is one of Ovid’s charming aetiologies — origin stories for everyday things. The “eyes” on a peacock’s tail feathers are the hundred eyes of dead Argus, placed there by Juno. The peacock was Juno’s sacred bird, as the eagle was Jupiter’s. Ovid knew his reader would picture a peacock spreading its tail — that flash of recognition between myth and the everyday world is exactly what he was after.

✅ TRY IT · the aetiology
According to Ovid, the “eyes” on a peacock’s tail are…

Commit first: line 5 has Iō, ā custōde circumdata, in agrīs errābat. What type is circumdata, and how would you translate the phrase? Say it, then reveal.

PPP (nom. sg. f. of circumdō): passive, prior to the main verb. The ā custōde (“by the guard”) is the passive agent. Translation: “Io, surrounded by the guard, was wandering in the fields.”
