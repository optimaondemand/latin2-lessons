💡 THREE WORDS TO WATCH

tangō has an irregular perfect stem (tetingī) and a PPP tactus, -a, -um — the stem shifts. manus, -ūs is 4th declension feminine (like frūctus but feminine); remember that for PPP agreement. famēs, famis is 3rd declension — nominative and accusative are regular.

📚 PART 2 · TWO READING SKILLS 10 min

Skill 1: the perfect passive has two translations

Tactus est can be “was touched” (simple past) or “has been touched” (present perfect). Both are correct — context decides which reads better. Latin does not distinguish these two English tenses.

Cibus ā Mīdā tactus est.

The food was touched by Midas. or The food has been touched by Midas. — tactus agrees with cibus (m. sg.).

Skill 2: one small word tells the two tenses apart

Perfect passive

PPP + est / sunt

cibus tactus est
the food was touched

Pluperfect passive

PPP + erat / erant

cibus tactus erat
the food had been touched

The pluperfect describes an action completed before another past action: omnia ā rēge optāta erant — “everything had been wished for by the king” (before he realized his error). Same participle rule; just imperfect esse.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
Which of the two tenses feels trickier to you so far?

Give both English translations of dōnum datum est, then say what the PPP datum agrees with. Think it through, then reveal.

“The gift was given” or “the gift has been given” — both correct. datum is neuter singular nominative, agreeing with dōnum (n. sg.); est is the 3rd sg. present of esse, marking the perfect passive.
🏛️ STORY CONNECTION · THE GRAMMAR OF TRANSFORMATION

Everything Midas touches is transformed (mūtātur). But once the golden touch has happened, the result is described with the perfect passive: cibus aurō mūtātus est — the food was turned to gold. The PPP + est captures the completed transformation, with the golden state now visible and permanent. Midas cannot undo what tactus est. The perfect passive is the grammar of irreversible change.
