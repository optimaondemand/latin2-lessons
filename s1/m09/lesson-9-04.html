⏰ PART 1 · READ THE PASSAGE 16 min

Aureus Mīdas

Read aloud, one line at a time. The bold forms are the perfect and pluperfect passives — pause at each and ask: what is the subject, and does the participle agree? Use the glossary below for anything unfamiliar.

AFTER OVID, METAMORPHŌSĒS XI · ADAPTED FOR LATIN 2
1Silenus, comes Bacchī, in agrīs Phrygiīs errābat. Ā Mīdā rēge inventus est et ad rēgem ductus est.
2Mīdās Silenum bene accēpit et post decem diēs ad Bacchum redūxit. Bacchus rēgem laude dignum esse crēdidit.
3Bacchus Mīdāe dīxit: “Dōnum optā, ō rēx — quodcumque volueris dābitur.” Mīdās statim respondit: “Id optō ut omnia quae tangō aurō fīant.”
4Dōnum dātum est. Mīdās laetus domum īvit et omnia tangere coepīt.
5Cibus ā rēge tactus est: statim aureus factus est. Vīnum tactum est: statim aurum in cupā erat.
6Mīdās miserē flēbat. Omnia quae optāverat iam mūtāta erant: mens stulta rēgis nunc clāra erat.
7Famēs rēgem vexābat. Cibus tactus erat antequam rēx comedere potuit: nunc aureus et dūrus erat.
8Mīdās ad fluvium Pactōlum īvit et in aquā manūs lāvit. Dōnum stultum ā rēge relictum est — sed aurum in fluvium trānslātum est et ibi semper manet.
Glossary:  comes — companion (nom. sg. of comes, comitis) · errābat — was wandering (impf. of errō) · inventus est — was found (perf. pass. of inveniō; PPP inventus, m. sg.) · ductus est — was led (perf. pass. of dūcō) · laude dignum — worthy of praise · quodcumque volueris — whatever you shall have wished (gloss only) · dābitur — will be given (fut. pass.) · ut…fīant — that (they) become (purpose clause — gloss only) · cupā — cup (abl. sg. of cupa, -ae) · Pactōlum — the river Pactolus in Lydia, said to have golden sands ever after · manūs lāvit — washed his hands (perf. of lavō) · trānslātum est — was transferred (perf. pass. of trānsferō; n. sg.)
📚 PART 2 · CULTURE 6 min
🏛️ THE RIVER PACTOLUS · A MYTH THAT EXPLAINS A FACT

The Greeks and Romans explained the gold-bearing sands of the river Pactolus in Lydia (modern Turkey) through this very story. When Midas washed away the golden touch in the Pactolus, the river's sands turned golden. This is an aetiology — a myth that explains a real-world phenomenon. The Pactolus was indeed rich in gold alluvium, and that wealth helped fund the kingdom of Lydia, which produced some of the world's first coinage.

In your notes, translate the three sentences below, name each tense (perfect or pluperfect), and say what the PPP agrees with. Think it through, then reveal.

1. Cibus ā Mīdā tactus est.
2. Omnia ā rēge optāta erant.
3. Nōnne rēx ā fātō pūnītus est?
1. “The food was touched by Midas.” — perfect passive; tactus (m. sg. nom.) agrees with cibus.
2. “Everything had been wished for by the king.” — pluperfect passive; optāta (n. pl. nom.) agrees with omnia; erant = impf. pl. of esse.
3. “Surely the king was punished by fate, wasn't he?” — perfect passive; pūnītus (m. sg. nom.) agrees with rēx; nōnne expects “yes.”
