💡 THREE THINGS TO NOTICE

morior is a deponent verb — its forms look passive (including its PPP mortuus) but its meaning is active: “to die,” not “to be died.” rēs, reī is the module's one new declension class (5th). frūctus is 4th declension, like arcus from Latin 1.

📌 PART 2 · WHAT THE PPP MEANS 12 min

A state that results from a finished action

Rule 3 — meaning. A PPP describes a state that results from a completed passive action. velum cruentum = “the veil that has been bloodied” — or simply “the bloodstained veil.” The action (bloodying) is done; the condition (bloodstained) is visible now. English usually uses a single participial adjective: wounded, sent, conquered, loved.

Thisbē vulnerāta ex silvā fūgerat.

Thisbe, wounded, had fled from the forest. — vulnerāta = PPP, f. sg. nom., describing Thisbe by a completed wounding.

📜 RULE 4 · A PREVIEW (M09–M10)

When a PPP joins a form of esse, it builds the perfect or pluperfect passive tense: amātus est = “he was / has been loved” (perfect, M09); amātus erat = “he had been loved” (pluperfect, M10). For now, treat the PPP as a verbal adjective only — you will build the compound tenses in the next two modules.

🏛️ STORY CONNECTION · THE GRAMMAR OF EVIDENCE

Pyramus finds Thisbe's veil (velum), torn and bloodied (cruentum) by the lion. He reads the veil's condition as a completed action: Thisbe has been killed. The PPP captures exactly this — an action completed, its result visible in the present. When Pyramus sees velum cruentum, he is not watching an action happen; he is reading the aftermath of one.

What is your quickest test for telling a PPP from a regular adjective?

Commit first, then check: translate Pyramus velum cruentum vīdit, and say whether cruentum is a PPP. Write it down, then reveal.

“Pyramus saw the bloodstained veil.” Here cruentum is a regular adjective (cruentus, -a, -um), n. sg. acc., agreeing with velum — not a PPP, because no verb's fourth part produces it. It still shows the PPP meaning pattern: a visible result of a completed action.
