⏰ PART 1 · READ THE PASSAGE 16 min

Echō et Nārcissus

Read the passage aloud. As you go, find every bold passive verb and decide its conjugation, tense, person, and number. The glossary below handles the words and forms you haven't formally learned yet.

AFTER OVID, METAMORPHōSēS III · ADAPTED FOR LATIN 2
1Echō, nympha pulchra, ōlim nimis multā dīcēbat. Iūnō autem īrāta erat et Echōnem pūnīre volēbat.
2Iūnō ad nympham dīxit: “Vōx tua semper ā mē accūsātur! Ex hōc diē ultimae verbōrum aliōrum partes ā tē sōlum repetentur.”
3Sīc Echō ā deā pūnīta est. Iam tota vōx eius in silvīs audītur, sed verba propria dīcere nōn potest — sōlum ultima verba aliōrum repetī.
4In eīsdem silvīs Nārcissus, iuvenis pulcherrimus et superbus, per umbrās ambulābat. Omnēs nymphae eum amābant, sed ille nūllam amābat.
5Echō Nārcissum vīdit et statim amāvit. Nympha eum per silvam secūta est, sed tacēbat — prīma verba dīcere nōn poterat.
6Tum Nārcissus clāmāvit: “Ecquis adest?” Echō respondit: “Adest!” Nārcissus quaesīvit: “Hūc venī!” Echō iterāvit: “Venī!”
7Nārcissus Echōnem vidit et rīsit: “Nōlī mē tangere! Prius moriar quam ā tē tenēbor!”
8Echō ā Nārcissō repulsa est. Sōla manēbat in cavernīs montium et vōx eius adhūc in saxīs mittitur et ab omnibus audītur.
Glossary:  nimis multā — too many things (acc. pl.) · dīcēbat — was saying (imperfect active of dīcō, 3rd conj.) · īrāta — angry (nom. sg. f. adj.) · pūnīre volēbat — wanted to punish (volo + infin. of pūniō, 4th conj.) · ultimae — last, final (gen. sg. f.) · aliōrum — of others (gen. pl. of alius) · repetentur — will be repeated (future passive, 3rd conj. — preview, see M04) · pūnīta est — was punished (perfect passive — M08) · propria — her own (nom. pl. n. of proprius) · repetī — she repeated (perfect active of repetō) · iuvenis — young man (nom. sg.) · secūta est — followed (perfect of sequor, deponent) · poterat — she was able (imperfect of possum) · ecquis — who is there? (interrogative) · hūc — here, to this place · iterāvit — repeated (perfect active of iterō) · tangere — to touch (pres. infin. of tangō, 3rd conj.) · prius moriar quam — I will die before · repulsa est — was rejected (perfect passive of repellō) · cavernīs — caves (abl. pl. of caverna) · adhūc — still, even now · saxīs — rocks (abl. pl. of saxum, n.) · audītur — is heard (3rd sg. pres. passive of audiō, 4th conj. — preview form)
📜 PART 2 · THE STORY BEHIND THE WORDS 8 min
💬 ONE MYTH, TWO ENGLISH WORDS

Echo's story gave Latin, and then English, the word for reflected sound. The Greek ἠχώ (ēchō) meant “sound” or “noise”; after Ovid's retelling became the dominant version of the myth, the word became permanently tied to repeated sound. English borrowed it directly as echo, and it gave rise to echolocation, echocardiogram, and dozens of technical terms. The Narcissus half of the story gave English another Ovidian word: narcissism — excessive self-love, exactly the trait that drives Narcissus to his fate. Two words from a single myth cycle, still alive two thousand years later.

⚖️ THE PASSIVE AS FATE

Watch how the passive verbs cluster around Echo: her voice accūsātur (is accused), audītur (is heard), mittitur (is sent back from the rocks). She is the grammatical patient of her own story — things happen to her voice; she cannot make them happen. Narcissus, by contrast, fears only being held: tenēbor (I will be held). Even his one passive is a refusal.

Now try the reading task itself. In your notes, translate the three sentences below and identify each highlighted passive verb — conjugation, tense, person, number. Then reveal to check.

1. Echō ā Iūnōne accūsātur.
2. Nūntius ā duce mittitur.
3. Nympha formōsa mox ab omnibus laudābitur.
1. Echo is accused by Juno. accūsātur = 1st conj. present passive, 3rd sg (review from M01/M02). Stem accūsā- + -tur; Echō (nom.) is the subject.

2. A message is sent by the leader. mittitur = 3rd conj. present passive, 3rd sg (NEW M03). Stem mitt- + short -i- + -tur; Nūntius (nom.) is the subject.

3. The beautiful nymph will soon be praised by all. laudābitur = 1st conj. future passive, 3rd sg (NEW M03). Stem laudā- + future marker -bi- + -tur; Nympha (nom.) is the subject.
