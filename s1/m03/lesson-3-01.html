★ The Voice That Cannot Choose — Two New Passives
Lesson · about 35 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
You already know how to say something is done and was being done. This week you learn two new ways the passive can speak — and meet a nymph who has lost the power to speak for herself.
Modules 01 and 02 gave you the passive voice for 1st and 2nd conjugation verbs in the present and imperfect tenses. Today there is no paradigm to drill — just the map of the week: what the 3rd conjugation present passive and the future passive add to what you can already do, and why Ovid's story of Echo is the perfect image of passive speech.

📚 PART 2 · THE BIG PICTURE 14 min

Two new passive forms — and one old warning

Module 03 introduces two passive forms that test and extend everything you built in M01–M02.

New #1 — 3rd conj. present passive

mittitur
it is sent

The most challenging passive so far. Its stem vowel is short and shifts by ending — no stable long vowel. Model verb: mittō, mittere.

New #2 — future passive (1st & 2nd)

laudābitur
it will be praised

Follows the future active pattern you know: the -bi-/-b- marker stays; passive endings replace the active ones.

Because the 3rd conjugation vowel is short and unstable, you will learn those forms as a set rather than deriving each one mechanically. The future passive, by contrast, is a small change to something you already own.

💡 ONE LIMIT TO NOTE

The future passive this week applies only to 1st and 2nd conjugation. The 3rd conjugation future passive is formed differently (with -ē-, not -bi-) and is the main topic of Module 04. This week: 3rd conjugation present passive, and 1st/2nd conjugation future passive.

📜 PART 3 · THE STORY THIS WEEK 16 min

Echo, whose words are chosen for her

The story moves to Book III of Ovid's Metamorphōsēs. The nymph Echo, cursed by Juno to repeat only the last words she hears, meets the proud hunter Narcissus in the forest. Echo's voice — scattered, reflected, incomplete — is the perfect image of passive speech: she does not choose her words; they are chosen for her. That is exactly what the passive voice does grammatically — the subject does not act; it is acted upon.

In your own words: how is the grammar of this module (the passive voice) connected to the story of Echo? Think it through, then reveal one way to say it.

The passive voice is about a subject that receives an action rather than doing it. Echo is cursed so that she cannot originate her own speech — she can only repeat the words of others. She is, in effect, a person forced to live in the passive voice: acted upon, never acting. That is why Ovid's story is the ideal setting for a module on passive forms.
