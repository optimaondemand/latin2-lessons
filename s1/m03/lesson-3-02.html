Lesson 3.02
★ Sent, Not Sending — 3rd Conjugation Present Passive
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
The hardest passive paradigm so far — because for the first time the connecting vowel refuses to stay put.
In the 1st conjugation, the long ā appears consistently; in the 2nd, the long ē. In the 3rd conjugation, the vowel is short and varies by person. You cannot derive each form mechanically the way you could before — so today you learn the 3rd conjugation present passive as a set, using the model verb mittō, mittere (to send).
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to conjugate mittere in the present passive, all six persons.
2
I will be able to tell 3rd conjugation passive apart from 1st and 2nd by the vowel before -tur.
3
I will be able to explain why mitteris (short e) is not the same as 2nd conjugation monēris (long ē).

💡 QUICK COMPARISON · 3RD SINGULAR ACROSS CONJUGATIONS

amātur (1st) · monētur (2nd) · mittitur (3rd). The long ā, long ē, and short i before -tur are the fastest way to identify a verb's conjugation at a glance in the passive voice.

Say the passive set aloud as one unit: mittor — mitteris — mittitur — mittimur — mittiminī — mittuntur. The short i and the -untur in the 3rd plural are the most characteristic features.

📌 PART 3 · THREE THINGS TO WATCH 8 min

The forms most likely to trip you

1. mitteris vs. monēris

The 2nd singular is the form most easily confused. monēris has a long ē (you are warned); mitteris has a short e (you are sent). The dictionary entry (monēre vs. mittere) resolves the ambiguity — always look up an unfamiliar verb.

2. The vowel pattern

mittor (no connector, 1sg), mitteris (short e, 2sg), mittitur (short i, 3sg), mittimur (short i, 1pl), mittiminī (short i, 2pl), mittuntur (connecting -u-, 3pl). Memorize the full set.
💡 3. THE 1ST SINGULAR IS THE EASY ONE

mittor follows the same rule as all conjugations: the active -ō drops and -r is added. You know this already from amor and moneor: amor (I am loved) · moneor (I am warned) · mittor (I am sent).

One more, out loud: in the sentence Nūntius ā duce mittitur, identify mittitur fully — conjugation, tense, voice, person, and number — and translate. Say your answer, then reveal.

“A message is sent by the leader.” mittitur = 3rd conjugation, present tense, passive voice, 3rd person singular. Stem mitt- + short -i- + -tur. Nūntius (nominative) is the subject that receives the action.
