📚 PART 2 · THE FUTURE PASSIVE 16 min

Keep the marker, swap the ending

Formation: present stem + -bi- (future marker) + passive personal endings. Meaning: “will be [verb]ed.” Here are both conjugations side by side.

Person1st Conj. (amō)Meaning2nd Conj. (moneō)Meaning
1st sgamāborI will be lovedmonēborI will be warned
2nd sgamāberisyou will be lovedmonēberisyou will be warned
3rd sgamābiturhe/she/it will be lovedmonēbiturhe/she/it will be warned
1st plamābimurwe will be lovedmonēbimurwe will be warned
2nd plamābiminīyou (pl.) will be lovedmonēbiminīyou (pl.) will be warned
3rd plamābunturthey will be lovedmonēbunturthey will be warned

Rule 1 — future stem + passive ending

If you know the active future, just change the personal ending. amābit (he will love) → amābitur (he will be loved). The -bi- marker does not change; only the ending shifts.

Rule 2 — the 1st singular is -bor

Three 1st-singular passives to keep apart: present amor (no b), imperfect amābar (-bār), future amābor (-bor). The vowel before -r is the key: ā = imperfect, o = future.
💡 PREVIEW · 3RD CONJUGATION FUTURE PASSIVE

The future passive of 3rd, 4th, and 3rd-iō verbs is formed differently: instead of -bi-, they use -ē- + passive endings (e.g., mittar, mitteris, mittetur…). That is the main topic of Module 04. For now, the -bi-/-bor pattern works for 1st and 2nd conjugation future passives only.

💡 NOTES ON THE HARDER ENTRIES

mittō is the model verb for yesterday's 3rd conjugation passive — learn it well. taceō is 2nd conjugation (a familiar pattern). fōns, fontis is a 3rd-declension i-stem noun; its genitive singular is fontis. ōs, ōris is a 3rd-declension neuter; its nominative/accusative plural is ōra.

One more, out loud: how would you distinguish imperfect passive laudābātur from future passive laudābitur at a glance? Say your rule, then reveal.
