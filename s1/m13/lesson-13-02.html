📚 PART 2 · THE REFERENCE CARD 16 min

All six passive tenses at a glance

Here is the complete passive system for a model verb (amō, amāre), shown in the 3rd person singular. Study the last column — the signal — hardest of all. It is what your eye should catch first.

Tense3rd sg. formMeaningSignal
Present passiveamāturhe/she is lovedpresent stem + passive ending
Imperfect passiveamābāturhe/she was being loved-bā- + passive ending
Future passiveamābiturhe/she will be loved-bi- + passive ending
Perfect passiveamātus esthe/she was / has been lovedPPP + est / sunt
Pluperfect passiveamātus erathe/she had been lovedPPP + erat / erant
Future perfect passiveamātus erithe/she will have been lovedPPP + erit / erunt
💡 TWO HALVES, TWO METHODS

The first three tenses (present, imperfect, future) are one word built on the present stem — look for -bā- or -bi-, or neither. The last three (perfect, pluperfect, future perfect) are two words — a PPP plus a form of esse. The tense of esse tells you which one: est → perfect, erat → pluperfect, erit → future perfect.

Commit before you look: in the passage you will meet posita erat and aedificātum erat. Say what tense both are, and why. Then reveal.

Pluperfect passive. Both are PPP (posita, aedificātum) plus erat — the imperfect of esse. PPP + erat/erant always signals the pluperfect passive: “had been placed / had been built.”
