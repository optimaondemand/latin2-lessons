⏰ PART 1 · THE PASSAGE 18 min

Phaethōn ad Sōlem Iter Facit

Phaethon has been mocked by a classmate who denied that Sol was truly his father. Furious and desperate, he travels to the eastern edge of the world to find his father’s palace and demand proof — and more. Read for meaning first; then go back and mark every passive verb (name its tense) and every PAP (attributive or circumstantial).

After Ovid, Metamorphōsēs II — adapted for Latin 2
1Phaethōn, fīlius Sōlis esse dīcēbātur; sed ā sociīs rīdēbātur et nōn crēdēbātur.
2Puer, ā sociīs superbus appellātus, ad patrem suum iter fēcit.
3Palātium Sōlis, in orientē positum, ex aurō et gemmīs aedificātum erat.
4Phaethōn, iānuam auream vidēns, attonitus substitit; tāle aedificium numquam vīderat.
5Sōl, in soliō aureō sedēns, Phaethontem vīdit et agnōvit.
6“Cūr hūc venīstī?” inquit Sōl. “Ā multīs hominĭbus haec via nōn tentātur.”
7Phaethōn, precēs suās dīcēns, ōrāvit: “Ō pater, currus tuus mihi dābātur in somnīs!”
8Sōl autem movēbātur precĭbus fīliī; iūrāvit per Stygem sē quidquid Phaethōn vellet datūrum esse.
9“Equī meī nōn regentur ā tē,” inquit Sōl; “iter arduum est et ā deīs ipsīs timētur.”
10Phaethōn, verba patris nōn audiēns, īnstābat: currus Sōlis ā sē agī dēbēbat.
Glossary:  dīcēbātur — was said to be (impf. pass. of dīcō) · crēdēbātur — was believed (impf. pass.) · appellātus — having been called (PPP of appellō) · orientē — the east (abl.) · positum / aedificātum erat — had been placed / built (plupf. pass.) · iānuam — door, gate (acc.) · soliō — throne (abl.) · agnōvit — recognized (perf. act.) · tentātur — is attempted (pres. pass.) · in somnīs — in dreams · per Stygem — by the Styx (the unbreakable divine oath) · datūrum esse — that he would give (fut. act. inf., indirect statement) · regentur — will be ruled (fut. pass. of regō) · timētur — is feared (pres. pass.) · īnstābat — was insisting (impf. of īnstō) · agī dēbēbat — ought to be driven (pass. inf. of agō with dēbēbat)
📚 PART 2 · THE STORY BEHIND IT 10 min
🏛️ HYBRIS AND THE UNBREAKABLE OATH

Sol’s fateful mistake is swearing by the Styx before he hears what Phaethon wants. The Stygian oath is the one divine promise that cannot be broken — not even by Jupiter. Ovid frames it as a moment of parental love overriding divine prudence.

When Phaethon finally reveals that he wants to drive the sun-chariot, Sol cannot refuse. The rest of Metamorphōsēs II is the disaster that follows. Notice how Ovid’s Phaethon is not simply villainous — he is a son desperate to prove his parentage, and his superbia is understandable even as it leads to catastrophe.

⚖️ PASSIVE AND PARTICIPLE, TOGETHER

Watch the double layer at work. The passive verbs (rīdēbātur, movēbātur, regentur, timētur) show what happens to Phaethon and around him. The participles (vidēns, sedēns, dīcēns, audiēns) keep every figure in motion at the moment of the main verb. Reading both at once is the whole art of reading Ovid.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
Which line gave you the most trouble on the first read?

Commit before you look: fully translate line 9 (“Equī meī nōn regentur ā tē… iter arduum est et ā deīs ipsīs timētur”) and name the tense of each passive verb. Then reveal.

“My horses will not be controlled by you; the journey is steep and is feared even by the gods themselves.” — regentur is future passive (3rd pl. of regō; subject equī, agent ā tē); timētur is present passive (3rd sg. of timeō; agent ā deīs).
