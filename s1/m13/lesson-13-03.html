⏰ PART 1 · THE PARTICIPLE CARD 12 min

Present active participle at a glance

The PAP is active: it describes an ongoing action of the noun it modifies, at the time of the main verb. It is a 3rd-declension adjective, so it agrees in case, number, and gender — and, because it is verbal, it can take its own object.

FeatureRuleExample
FormationPresent stem + -ns, -ntiscurrēns, currentis
AgreementCase, number, gender of the noun it modifiescanem currentem (acc. sg.)
Attributive useModifies a noun directly, like an adjectivecanis currēns (the running dog)
Circumstantial useAnchors a verbal phrase; may take its own objectPhaethōn, iter petēns, vēnit
TranslationSimultaneous / causal / temporal — pick the most naturalwhile seeking / because he sought / when he sought

Attributive

canis currēns — “the running dog.” The participle just labels the noun, like any adjective. No separate clause needed.

Circumstantial

Phaethōn, iter petēns, vēnit — “Phaethon, while seeking the road, came.” The participle heads a whole phrase (with its object iter) describing the circumstances of the main verb.
📚 PART 2 · TEN NEW WORDS 10 min

The vocabulary of the Sun-chariot

These ten words belong to the Phaethon narrative and recur throughout the myth. Currus is a 4th-declension noun; iter and prex are 3rd declension with slightly irregular stems. Learn the genitive — it appears in the passage.

LatinEnglish
sōl, sōlism. — sun; the god Sol
currus, -ūsm. — chariot (4th decl.)
equus, -īm. — horse
caelum, -īn. — sky, heaven
pater, patrism. — father
frēnum, -īn. — rein, bridle (pl. frēna)
iter, itinerisn. — journey, route
prex, precisf. — prayer, plea (usu. pl. precēs)
superbus, -a, -umproud, arrogant
arduus, -a, -umsteep, high, difficult
💡 WATCH THE STEMS

Iter has the surprising genitive itineris; prex gives precis and most often appears in the plural precēs, precum; currus (4th decl.) has genitive currūs. Superbus and arduus are ordinary 1st/2nd-declension adjectives — familiar territory from Latin 1.

Commit before you look: translate Phaethōn, precēs dīcēns, ōrāvit, and say whether dīcēns is attributive or circumstantial. Then reveal.

“Phaethon, uttering his prayers, pleaded.” Dīcēns is a circumstantial PAP: nom. sg. m, agreeing with Phaethōn, heading a phrase with its own object precēs. Translate it “while uttering / as he uttered” — the circumstance surrounding ōrāvit.
