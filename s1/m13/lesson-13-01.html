⏰ PART 1 · WHY NO NEW GRAMMAR? 8 min

Consolidation is a promotion, not a rest

You now know a great deal of Latin grammar. But knowing a form and recognizing it instantly in a real sentence are two different skills. Module 14 will add the future active participle; Module 15 will bring the whole participial system together. Before that step, the six passive tenses and the PAP need to be automatic — read and translated without stopping to think. Module 13 is the practice passage that makes that automaticity possible.

💡 WHAT “AUTOMATIC” MEANS

When you see amābātur, you should not have to reconstruct it — the -bā- should flash “imperfect” and the -tur should flash “passive.” That instant reading is the whole goal of this module.

📚 PART 2 · THE DOUBLE LAYER 10 min

Two grammars, one sentence

Phaethon’s story is the great Ovidian tale of ambition meeting fate. His claim that Sol is his father — his superbia (arrogance) — drives everything. Ovid tells it with two grammars at once:

Passive — what is done to him

Fate acts upon Phaethon: he is mocked, will be thrown, was struck, will be burned. The passive voice puts him on the receiving end.

PAP — what he is doing

At each moment he is in motion: iter petēns (seeking the road), precēs dīcēns (uttering prayers), verba nōn audiēns (not listening). The participle keeps him active.

Watch for both layers in the same sentence: the passive verb shows Phaethon being acted upon; the PAP shows him still moving toward his fate. That double layer is Ovid’s technique — and your reading target this week.
