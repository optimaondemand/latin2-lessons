Lesson 2.03
★ Was Being Changed — The Imperfect Passive
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
A transformation happens in stages — and Latin has a tense built for exactly that.
Today you add the imperfect passive for both 1st and 2nd conjugations, using the familiar -bā- infix you already know from the active imperfect. The meaning is “was being warned” or “used to be warned” — the perfect tense for describing Daphne's skin being covered by bark and her hair being changed into leaves. You will also meet the module's ten new words, all of which appear in tomorrow's reading.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…

2
I will be able to recognize the -bā- infix as the imperfect marker in both active and passive.
3
I will be able to recall the ten new words of Module 02.

💡 TWO ODDITIES WORTH NOTING

laurus is 2nd declension but feminine — like humus and most tree names. crīnis is a 3rd declension i-stem; its plural crīnēs (“hair, locks”) is common in this passage.

📚 PART 2 · THE IMPERFECT PASSIVE 14 min

Stack the pieces you already own

You know the active imperfect well: amābam, amābās, amābat… The marker is the -bā- infix between stem and ending. The imperfect passive works the same way — the -bā- appears, but passive endings follow. Formation: present stem + -bā- + passive ending.

Person1st conj. (amō)2nd conj. (moneō)Meaning
1st sgamābarmonēbarI was being warned
2nd sgamābārismonēbārisyou were being warned
3rd sgamābāturmonēbāturhe/she/it was being warned
1st plamābāmurmonēbāmurwe were being warned
2nd plamābāminīmonēbāminīyou (pl.) were being warned
3rd plamābanturmonēbanturthey were being warned

Active imperfect — ends -bam

amābam — I was loving

The 1st sg ends in -m.

Passive imperfect — ends -bar

amābar — I was being loved

The 1st sg ends in -r, not -bor.
💡 TWO INDEPENDENT LAYERS

amābātur = amā- (stem) + bā (imperfect) + -tur (passive). The tense signal and the voice signal are separate; read them one at a time.
