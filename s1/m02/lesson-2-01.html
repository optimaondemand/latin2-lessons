Lesson 2.01
★ The Passive Returns — Now in the Second Conjugation
Lesson · about 35 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
You already know the passive. This week you learn to hear it in a second voice — and to read the moment a pursued girl becomes a tree.
In Module 01 you met the passive voice through the first conjugation: amātur, “he is loved.” Latin swapped its active endings for a new passive set — -or, -ris, -tur, -mur, -minī, -ntur — and those endings do not change. Today there is nothing to drill: just one big idea. What changes from conjugation to conjugation is only the stem vowel in front of those endings. This week's story — Daphne's transformation into the laurel — is soaked in the passive, because Daphne is held, moved, and changed by forces beyond her.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to recall the six passive personal endings and explain that they stay the same across conjugations.
2
I will be able to predict that a second conjugation passive keeps those endings but carries a long ē stem.
⏰ PART 1 · WARM-UP 6 min

What the passive already gave you

The passive voice shows the subject receiving the action instead of doing it. Compare the two sentences below — same actors, opposite direction.

Apollo Daphnen amat.

Apollo loves Daphne. — active: Apollo does the loving.

Daphne ab Apolline amātur.

Daphne is loved by Apollo. — passive: Daphne receives the loving. (amātur = 1st conj. present passive, 3rd sg.)

📚 PART 2 · THE BIG IDEA 10 min

Same endings, new stem

Here is the one idea that carries the whole module. The six passive endings never change. What changes from conjugation to conjugation is the stem vowel in front of them. First conjugation wears a long ā; second conjugation wears a long ē.

1st conjugation — long ā

amātur
he/she/it is loved

Stem amā- + passive ending.

2nd conjugation — long ē

monētur
he/she/it is warned

Stem monē- + the very same ending.

Read them side by side: amātur and monētur. The ending -tur is identical. Only the vowel before it tells you which family you are in. Learn to check that vowel, and half the module is done.

💡 TWO TENSES THIS WEEK

You will build the passive in two tenses: the present passive (learned tomorrow) and the imperfect passive (“was being warned”), which uses the familiar -bā- infix you already know from the active imperfect. Both tenses use the same passive endings — you are only stacking pieces you have already met.

One prediction before you go: the verb teneō, tenēre means “to hold” and is 2nd conjugation. Using only the big idea from today, predict its 3rd person singular present passive (“he/she/it is held”). Say it, then reveal.

tenētur — “he/she/it is held.” The passive ending -tur is exactly the same as in 1st conjugation; the long ē stem (tenē-) is what marks it as 2nd conjugation. You just built a form you haven't formally learned yet.
