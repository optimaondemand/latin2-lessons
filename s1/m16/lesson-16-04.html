⏰ PART 1 · READ THE PASSAGE 16 min

Iō Aegyptum Pervenit

After Ovid, Metamorphōsēs I — Adapted for Latin 2

Read each line aloud first. Find the subject and verb, spot every participle and passive form, then translate. The glossary below the passage supports the harder words.

1Iō, ā Iūnōne odiō habitā, per multās terrās asilō vexābātur, neque quiētem invenīre poterat.
2Dea saeva, Iōnem semper persequēns, asilum mīserat ut nympha miseranda numquam quiēsceret.
3Iō, per montēs silvāsque agitāta, ad lītus maris pervēnit, ubi undae magnae audiēbantur.
4Ibi in herbā iacēns, Iō ad caelum oculōs suōs sustulit et Iovem lacrimīs ōrāvit.
5Iuppiter, Iōnem gementem in terrā prōstrātam vidēns, Iūnōnem ōrāre coepit.
6Iuppiter Iūnōnī prōmīsit: “Iō numquam ā mē amābitur; dā eī fōrmam suam.”
7Iūnō, ā Iove reconciliāta, tandem cōnsēnsit: mūnus clēmentiāe Iovī datum est.
8Iō, in fōrmam suam restitūta, exsultāvit: vōx hūmāna et corpus pulchrum eī reddita sunt.
9In Aegyptum perducta, Iō rēgīna eius terrae ā populō appellābātur.
10Postrēmō Iō dea facta, Isis appellāta est: nunc in ārīs Aegyptī ā multīs populīs colitur et laudātur.
Glossary:  odiō habitā — held in hatred (idiom: odio habere = to hate) · quiētem — rest, peace (acc. sg. of quiēs, quiētis) · persequēns — pursuing relentlessly (PAP nom. sg. f. of persequor) · miseranda — pitiable, to be pitied (gerundive used as adjective) · quiēsceret — might rest (subjunctive in a purpose clause — note for S2) · agitāta — driven, harried (PPP nom. sg. f. of agitō) · iacēns — lying (PAP nom. sg. f. of iaceō) · sustulit — raised, lifted (perfect of tollō) · lacrimīs — with tears (instrumental ablative) · gementem — groaning (PAP acc. sg. f. of gemō) · prōstrātam — prostrated, thrown down (PPP acc. sg. f. of prōsternō) · coepit — began (perfect of coepī) · reconciliāta — having been reconciled (PPP nom. sg. f. of reconciliō) · tandem — at last, finally · clēmentiāe — of mercy, clemency (gen. sg. of clēmentia) · exsultāvit — rejoiced, leapt for joy (perfect of exsultō) · reddita sunt — were restored, were given back (perfect passive of reddō) · perducta — having been led (PPP nom. sg. f. of perdūcō) · appellābātur — was called, was named (imperfect passive of appellō) · postrēmō — finally, at last · ārīs — altars (dat./abl. pl. of āra, -ae) · colitur — is worshipped, is honored (present passive of colō)
📚 PART 2 · CULTURAL READING 8 min
🏛️ ISIS AND IŌ

The identification of Iō with the Egyptian goddess Isis was a real feature of Greco-Roman religious thought, not just an Ovidian invention. Both figures were associated with the moon, with wandering, and with transformation. The cow was sacred to Isis, which made the parallel easy to draw. By the time Ovid was writing (late 1st century BCE / early 1st century CE), the cult of Isis had spread throughout the Roman world and was one of the most popular mystery religions in Italy. For Roman readers, the final lines of Iō's story would have carried genuine religious resonance.

✅ TRY IT · comprehension

Label each bolded form, then reveal. (1) Iō, ā Iūnōne vexāta, per terrās errābat.  (2) Iuppiter, Iōnem vidēns, Iūnōnem ōrāre coepit.  (3) Iō, in fōrmam suam restitūta, rēgīna Aegyptī erat.

1. Io, tormented by Juno, was wandering through the lands. — vexāta is PPP nom. sg. f.; ā Iūnōne = personal agent construction.
2. Jupiter, seeing Io, began to plead with Juno. — vidēns is PAP nom. sg. m. of videō, simultaneous with coepit.
3. Io, restored to her own form, was queen of Egypt. — restitūta is PPP nom. sg. f. of restituō, action completed before erat.
