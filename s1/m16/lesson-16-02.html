📚 PART 2 · CARD 1 — THE SIX PASSIVE TENSES 14 min

Every way Latin says “it was done to the subject”

All six passive indicative tenses, shown as the 3rd singular of amō, amāre. The stem + tense-sign + passive-ending pattern applies across all conjugations.

Tense3rd Sg PassiveTranslationKey marker
Presentamāturhe/she/it is lovedpresent stem + passive ending
Imperfectamābāturhe/she/it was being loved-bā- tense sign + passive ending
Futureamābiturhe/she/it will be loved-bi- tense sign + passive ending
Perfectamātus esthe/she/it was / has been lovedPPP + present of esse
Pluperfectamātus erathe/she/it had been lovedPPP + imperfect of esse
Future Perfectamātus erithe/she/it will have been lovedPPP + future of esse
💡 THE GREAT DIVIDE

The three present-system tenses (present, imperfect, future) are single words built on the stem with passive endings. The three perfect-system tenses (perfect, pluperfect, future perfect) are two words: the PPP plus a form of esse. Spot which of esse is used (est / erat / erit) and the tense names itself.

📚 PART 3 · CARD 2 — THE THREE PARTICIPLES 12 min

Verbal adjectives, sorted by voice and time

All three participles are verbal adjectives: they agree with a noun in gender, number, and case. They differ only in voice and time.

ParticipleAbbr.VoiceTimeForm (nom. sg. m.)Meaning
Perfect PassivePPPPassiveBefore main verbamātushaving been loved
Present ActivePAPActiveSame time as main verbamānsloving, while loving
Future ActiveFAPActiveAfter main verbamātūrusabout to love, intending to love
📜 HOW THEY DECLINE

All participles decline like adjectives. PPP: 1st/2nd declension (-us/-a/-um). PAP: 3rd declension consonant stem (-ns, gen. -ntis). FAP: 1st/2nd declension (-ūrus/-ūra/-ūrum).

Out loud: identify the form amātūrus — which participle is it, and what does it mean? Say your reasoning, then reveal.

FAP (future active participle), nom. sg. m. of amō — “about to love” or “intending to love.” The -ūrus ending is the giveaway: active voice, and time after the main verb.
