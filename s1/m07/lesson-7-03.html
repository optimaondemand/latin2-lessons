💡 TWO WORDS TO KEEP APART

mūrus (long ū) is “wall.” mōrus (long ō) is “mulberry tree.” They differ by one vowel — and both matter in this story. Also note: cōnstituō is a compound of statuō (perfect stem cōnstitu-); conveniō is a compound of veniō (perfect stem convēn-).

📚 PART 2 · PUT THE TENSE ON THE WORDS 8 min

The plan, in future perfect

Here is how the lovers' plan sounds when each step is a completed future action. Notice the perfect stem in each verb, then the future perfect ending.

Cum nox advēnerit, ad arborem conveniēmus.

When night will have come, we will meet at the tree. — advēnerit = stem advēn- + -erit (fut. perf.); conveniēmus = simple future that follows.

Cum signum dedērō, statim veniēs.

When I will have given the signal, you will come at once. — dedērō = perfect stem ded- (from dō, dare, dedī) + -erō.

Postquam omnia cōnstituērimus, iter faciēmus.

After we will have arranged everything, we will make the journey. — cōnstituērimus = stem cōnstitu- + -erimus.
