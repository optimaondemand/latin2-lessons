★ Building the Future Perfect
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Yesterday: the idea. Today: the forms — and they cost you almost nothing new.
The future perfect is built on the same perfect stem you already use for the pluperfect. Only the endings change: instead of -eram, -erās, -erat… you add -erō, -eris, -erit… Learn one paradigm here and it works for every Latin verb in the language.

Perfect stem + future-looking endings

💡 A MEMORY HOOK

The future perfect endings are almost the future tense of sum attached to the perfect stem: erō, eris, erit, erimus, eritis… (with -erint in the 3rd plural). Think amāv- + “I will be” = amāverō, “I will have loved.”

📌 PART 3 · THREE RULES 8 min

What to hold onto

RULE 1 · PERFECT STEM + -erō, -eris, -erit…

Both pluperfect and future perfect are built on the perfect stem; only the endings differ. Pluperfect endings echo eram (was); future perfect endings echo erō (will be). Examples: convēnerimus (when we will have met), advēnerit (when he will have arrived), fūgerint (when they will have fled).

RULE 2 · USED IN CONDITIONS AND “WHEN/AFTER” CLAUSES

Latin uses the future perfect where English often uses the simple present: Cum advēneris, ibi erō — “When you arrive (lit. will have arrived), I will be there.” The completing action must finish first.

RULE 3 · DON'T CONFUSE -erit WITH -erat

Future perfect 3rd sg -erit has a short -i- before -t; pluperfect 3rd sg -erat has a long -ā-. So amāverit = “will have loved” (future), amāverat = “had loved” (past). In context, a future main verb pairs with the future perfect; a past main verb with the pluperfect.
